ResMerge: Residual Policy Learning and Merging for Continual Adaptation of Pre-trained Robot Policies

Anonymous Authors

ResMerge enables data- and parameter-efficient continual adaptation of pre-trained robot policies. Each new task is acquired demo-free with residual RL, and its residual is merged into a bounded expert pool based on Residual-Field Consistency (RFC).

Highlights

  1. We propose ResMerge, a two-stage framework for continual adaptation of pre-trained robot policies that learns mergeable residual policies on top of a frozen base policy—enabling robots to sequentially acquire new skills while retaining old ones, with no demonstrations and a constant parameter footprint.
  2. ResMerge operates via a Learn-and-Merge loop: in Learn, a LoRA-based residual policy is trained per task with residual RL on sparse rewards while the base policy is frozen; in Merge, RFC (Residual-Field Consistency)—a single scalar measuring directional and magnitudinal alignment of two residual action fields—decides whether the new residual is merged with an existing expert or kept as a new one.
  3. We show that conventional task-similarity metrics fail for residual merging, whereas RFC enables safe consolidation. ResMerge consistently improves forward transfer, reduces forgetting, and raises success rates on LIBERO across two backbones and on a real-world Franka robot.

Motivation

Consider a generalist robot policy deployed in a home or factory. The robot must continuously learn new tasks as they arrive sequentially, adapting to each one without degrading performance on tasks it already knows. Existing Parameter-Efficient Continual Fine-Tuning (PECFT) methods isolate per-task updates with adapters, but they (1) require substantial per-task demonstrations and (2) add one adapter per task, so both data and parameter cost grow linearly with the task stream.

ResMerge removes both bottlenecks: residual RL acquires each task demo-free, and RFC-guided parameter-space merging keeps the expert pool bounded—a similar task may need an opposing correction (unsafe to merge), while a different task may need an aligned one (safe to merge), which RFC detects directly in residual action space.

Method

ResMerge operates in two stages. In Learn, a LoRA-parameterized residual policy Rcand is trained on the new task via residual RL with the base policy πbase frozen; the executed action is at = πbase(ot) + α Rcand(ot). In Merge, the new residual is consolidated with the most compatible existing expert only when their Residual-Field Consistency (RFC) exceeds a safety threshold τ; otherwise it is registered as a new expert.

Why a residual-specific criterion? Residual policies are correction fields defined relative to a shared frozen controller, not full task behaviors. Two residuals solving very different tasks may apply similar corrections and merge safely, while residuals on similar tasks may apply opposing corrections and destructively interfere. RFC therefore operates directly on the residual action fields, combining a Direction Consistency (DC) term (cosine alignment) and a Magnitude Consistency (MC) term (bounded scale ratio): RFC = DC · MC ∈ [−1, 1]. Compatible LoRA experts are then merged layer-wise with magnitude-aware pruning, cross-task normalization, and RFC-weighted averaging, followed by lightweight post-merge fine-tuning.

Experimental Setup

  • Simulation: LIBERO benchmark (LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Object), each with 10 sequential long-horizon manipulation tasks; base policies pre-trained on LIBERO-90 before continual adaptation.
  • Backbones (deliberately heterogeneous): a 32M ViT-T BC policy and an 8B OpenVLA-OFT VLA in simulation, and an IMLE BC policy on the real robot. Only the base policy/encoder changes—the residual actor, RFC criterion, and merging operators are the same code path.
  • Baselines: Sequential SFT, Experience Replay (ER), PackNet, TAIL, DMPEL, and a Multi-Task oracle trained jointly on all tasks.
  • Metrics: Forward Transfer (FWT), Negative Backward Transfer (NBT), Area Under the success-rate Curve (AUC), Model Parameter Efficiency (MPE), and Data Cost.

Results

Main Results

On the larger OpenVLA-OFT backbone, ResMerge reaches FWT 77%/53% (Spatial/Object), NBT 2%/1%, and AUC 95%/87%—approaching the Multi-Task oracle (95%/94% AUC) without any per-task demonstrations, while the Seq. SFT (LoRA) IL baseline still suffers heavy forgetting (NBT 98%/70%). On the smaller ViT-T backbone, ResMerge attains the highest AUC (83%/79%) and the most negative NBT (−3%/−8%) among the evaluated baselines. Negative NBT indicates positive backward transfer induced by RFC-based selective merging.

Ablations

Merging strategy (LIBERO-Object, ViT-T): RFC-gated selective merging is the only strategy that consolidates parameters without destructive interference—no-merge fails to consolidate and merge-all destroys via interference. Residual architecture: both MLP and LoRA residuals adapt equally well in the Learn stage (36% SR), but only LoRA survives merging (78% vs. 14% Merge-SR), because the low-rank constraint concentrates each task's adaptation in a structured subspace.

Analysis — RFC vs. Task-Similarity Merging

We visualize the RFC trajectory along the real-robot four-task sequence. Each panel compares the two residual action-correction fields being evaluated: streamlines trace the per-state direction of each field, the background encodes local direction consistency (green: aligned, red: opposed), and the box reports RFC statistics over 2,000 sampled states. (a) After R1, R2: fields are co-aligned (mean DC 0.81) ⇒ merge accepted ⇒ RAB. (b) After R3: RAB and R3 disagree almost everywhere (mean DC 0.01) ⇒ merge rejected, R3 kept separate. (c) After R4: fields realign (mean DC 0.50) ⇒ merge accepted ⇒ RCD. The final pool {RAB, RCD} reproduces the hardware-level grouping exactly.

Real-world Continual Learning

We deploy ResMerge on a Franka Emika Panda with a 16-DoF Leap dexterous hand and a single front-mounted RealSense D435 camera, with a frozen IMLE BC base policy. The robot sequentially learns four tabletop tasks in two paired groups, giving a hardware-level ground truth for merging: (T1) Pinch-Cube and (T2) Pinch-Apple pinch-grasp a small object into the box; (T3) Power-Steel and (T4) Power-Plastic power-grasp a cup onto the plate. Within each group tasks share grasp primitive and container but differ in object appearance, geometry, and friction; across groups both change.

ResMerge attains FWT 50%, NBT 7%, AUC 75%, outperforming DMPEL on both stability and overall performance. RFC selectively merges (T1,T2) into RAB and (T3,T4) into RCD, so the final pool contains two consolidated experts rather than four—matching the hardware-level grouping. Notably, RFC isolates residual transferability from base-policy capability: raw success rate would wrongly suggest R1 transfers better to the out-of-group T3,T4 than to its in-group partner T2; measuring similarity in residual action space recovers R1∼R2 as the actually-mergeable pair.
